History

History and safety

From the first championship in 1950 to today, and how hard moments made racing safer.

A short timeline

Some of these events involve real people who were injured or died. The timeline lists them as facts and shows what changed afterwards.

  1. 1950

    The first World Championship season. The opening race is held at Silverstone in Britain.

  2. 1976

    Niki Lauda crashes at the Nürburgring and suffers serious burns. He survives and returns to racing in the same season.

  3. 1994

    At Imola, Roland Ratzenberger and Ayrton Senna lose their lives. The sport begins a major safety programme afterwards.

  4. 2003

    The HANS device, which supports the head and neck, becomes mandatory.

  5. 2014

    The hybrid era begins with turbocharged V6 power units. Jules Bianchi is seriously injured at the Japanese Grand Prix.

  6. 2015

    The Virtual Safety Car is introduced to slow cars safely when marshals work on the track.

  7. 2018

    The Halo, a protective bar above the cockpit, becomes mandatory.

  8. 2020

    Romain Grosjean survives a heavy crash and fire in Bahrain. The Halo is widely credited with protecting him.

  9. 2022

    New cars return to ground-effect aerodynamics, designed to allow closer racing.

  10. 2026

    New rules bring power units with much more electrical power, active aerodynamics and fully sustainable fuel.

How safety improved

Cockpit

The Halo

A strong bar above the driver's head that protects against large objects and other cars.

Driver

The HANS device

A support worn on the shoulders that limits how far the head moves in a crash.

Race

Safety car and VSC

Both slow the field after an incident, so marshals and medical crews can work safely.
